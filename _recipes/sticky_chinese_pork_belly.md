## For the pork belly

---

* `1kg` rindless pork belly slices, chopped in half (each piece about the length of your index finger)
* `1L` hot chicken stock
* `1 thumb-sized piece` ginger, peeled and minced (or 1 tbsp ginger puree)
* `3 cloves` garlic, peeled and roughly chopped
* `1 tbsp` rice wine
* `1 tbsp` caster sugar

> Add the pork belly slices, stock, ginger, garlic, rice wine, and sugar to a heavy-based pan (a cast iron casserole pan works well).
>
> Bring to the boil, then place a lid on, turn down the heat and simmer for 2 hours.
>
> Turn off the heat, remove the pork from the pan and pat it dry using kitchen towels. The liquid can be reserved if you like (perfect for a Thai or Chinese noodle soup).
>
> Chop the pork into bite sized chunks.

## For the glaze

---

* `2 tbsp` vegetable oil, divided
* `pinch` salt and pepper, plus another pinch for frying
* `1 thumb-sized piece` ginger, peeled and minced
* `1` red chilli, finely chopped
* `2 tbsp` honey
* `2 tbsp` brown sugar
* `3 tbsp` dark soy sauce
* `1 tsp` lemon grass paste
* Chopped spring onions, to serve
* Chopped red chillies, to serve

> In a small bowl, mix together 1 tbsp of the oil, a pinch of salt and pepper, the ginger, chilli, honey, brown sugar, soy sauce, and lemongrass paste.
>
> Add the remaining 1 tbsp of oil to a frying pan and heat over a medium-high heat.
>
> Add the pork, along with a pinch of salt and pepper, and fry, turning regularly, until the pork starts to turn golden.
>
>>> The oil can splatter at this stage. A splatter guard is recommended.
>
> Pour the glaze over the pork and continue to cook for a couple of minutes, turning the pork often, until the pork looks dark and sticky.
>
> Remove from the heat and serve topped with spring onions and chopped chillies.
