## Giouvetsi

---

* `800g` chuck beef, cut into 3cm cubes
* `½ tsp` salt
* `¼ tsp` pepper
* `¼ tsp` allspice
* `¼ tsp` nutmeg
* `2 tbsp` olive oil
* `½` red onion, finely chopped
* `2 cloves` garlic, finely chopped
* `3 tbsp` tomato paste, be generous
* `60ml` red wine, optional
* `300g` crushed canned tomatoes
* `500ml` beef stock
* `1L` boiling water, divided
* `3` bay leaves
* `2 cups` orzo pasta (250ml Australian cups)
* Grated kefalotyri cheese or parsley, to serve

> Clean and pat dry the beef. Cut away excess fat and sinew and cut into 3cm cubes. Add to a bowl and season with the salt, pepper, allspice, and nutmeg. Mix to coat.
>
> Heat a large deep pot on high heat. Add the olive oil and cook the beef in 3 batches until browned on both sides. Return all the beef to the pot along with the onion and garlic. Cook for 1 minute, then add the tomato paste. Stir until the tomato paste starts caramelizing on the bottom.
>
> Add the red wine, beef stock, 500ml of the boiling water, and the bay leaves. Mix to combine. Place the lid on top, bring to a simmer, then lower to low heat and cook for 90 minutes. The beef should be tender and fall apart.
>
> Add the remaining 500ml boiling water and the orzo and stir. Cook for a further 20 minutes. Sprinkle with grated kefalotyri cheese or parsley and serve hot.

---

> Chuck beef is cheap and, because this dish is slow cooked, comes out fall-apart tender. Other cuts you can use include chuck roll or flatiron. You can even use chicken thigh.
>
> The cheapest red wine will work. If not using wine, replace it with more stock.
>
> Store in an airtight container in the fridge for up to three days.
